# Business models, pricing, go-to-market and growth economics of SMB hourly-workforce software (as of November 2026)

Scope note: this extends `docs/product/competitive-2026-10.md` (17 Oct 2026), which already holds a pricing table, feature matrix, app ratings and 2025-26 launches. Here the focus is money and growth: price and packaging, expansion revenue, channels, scale, funding and M&A, SMB SaaS benchmarks, and what this means for a one-customer entrant. Research date: 18 Nov 2026. "Primary" = vendor page or filing read on that date; "secondary" = trackers, blogs, review or aggregator sites (often conflicting). Older data is labelled with its year.

## 1. Pricing and packaging in 2026

### Takeaway
Two models dominate: per-location flat fees (Homebase $0-$120, 7shifts ~$40-$150, Connecteam $29-$119 per "hub") and per-user fees (When I Work $2.50-$8, Deputy $5-$9 with a $30/month minimum, Sling $0-$4, Square $4). Almost everyone runs a free tier capped at 5-30 staff and a 14-day no-card trial. The real money is in add-ons priced per location (tips, tasks) or per paid employee (payroll at ~$39-$49 base + $6/employee), with deep payroll promos ("6 months free") used to pull customers up-tier.

### Inferences
- Effective price for a typical 3-location, 45-employee coffee/QSR operator (15 staff per site): Homebase Essentials $90/mo (Plus $210); 7shifts Essentials ~$120-$135/mo; Deputy Core ~$293/mo; When I Work Essentials ~$113/mo (Pro $225); Connecteam Operations Basic $29-$35 (one hub covers 30 users, then 15 x $0.8-$1.0 = ~$41-$50 total); Square Shifts Plus ~$184/mo; Sling Premium ~$77-$90/mo. Adding payroll at ~$49 + $6 x 45 = ~$319/mo roughly doubles to triples spend. Payroll is therefore the single largest revenue line per customer for the vendors that own it.
- Per-location pricing (Homebase, 7shifts, Connecteam hubs) is easier for owners to predict and maps to how multi-unit operators budget; per-user pricing (Deputy, WIW, Square, Sling) scales with headcount and churns with seasonal staffing. For multi-location hourly businesses, per-location plus a per-employee payroll fee is the prevailing hybrid.
- Annual-prepay discounts cluster at 15-20% (Sling 15%, Connecteam 18%, Homebase 20%); minimums are small (Deputy $30, Square 2 members).

### Gaps
- 7shifts, Gusto and Rippling pricing pages could not be read directly; their numbers are secondary and conflicting.
- When I Work's Time & Attendance add-on price and annual prices are not shown on its page.
- No Workforce.com pricing found; no Puerto Rico-specific pricing or currency terms from any vendor.

## 2. Revenue expansion: payroll attach, fintech, tips, hiring, HR/compliance, AI, partner revenue

### Takeaway
The leaders make their real money after the scheduling sale: payroll (base + per employee, plus float interest), money movement (EWA, instant pay, tips, cards), hiring, insurance and retirement. Gusto shows the end-state (~$1B revenue with 401(k), banking and embedded payroll lines), Toast shows a POS platform folding labour into its bundle, and Homebase/7shifts show scheduling-first companies racing to attach payroll and on-demand pay.

- Homebase company materials cite "$3B in gross payroll processed" and 8 billion hours logged since founding (company figure surfaced on a third-party profile; unverified) — [yespress.io/homebase](https://yespress.io/homebase)

### Inferences
- Rough ARPA math: Toast subscription ARR / locations ≈ $1,279M / 189k ≈ $6,800 per location per year (~$560/month) vs. scheduling-only vendors at ~$25-$125/month per workplace (section 4). Owning payroll + payments is worth an order of magnitude more per location than owning the schedule.
- Payroll is attractive for three reasons: per-employee pricing that grows with the customer, float interest (~3% of revenue at Paychex scale), and very high switching costs. But every scheduling-first vendor except Homebase and 7shifts chose to partner (Deputy-Paycor, WIW-Rippling, Connecteam-integrations), which shows the tax/compliance build is expensive. Gusto, Homebase and 7shifts payroll are US-mainland only; the Puerto Rico payroll gap persists (Oct brief).
- EWA is a revenue line mainly through employee fees or interchange; the pattern among smaller vendors is to partner (Clair, Branch) rather than build. Given the trust problems in the Oct brief (fee anger) and regulation in flux, EWA is an expansion option to defer, not a launch feature.
- Tip management priced per location ($25-$50) is a clean upsell for hospitality and is highly relevant to drive-through coffee (tip pools).

### Gaps
- No vendor discloses payroll attach rates (share of scheduling customers who buy payroll) or EWA take rates/revenue.
- Homebase and 7shifts payroll revenue, interchange or float income are not public.
- Gusto's revenue mix (subscription vs. interest vs. partner) not found.

## 3. Go-to-market and distribution

### Takeaway
The winners combined (a) a free or cheap self-serve entry point that spreads through the employee app, (b) deep POS integrations and marketplace listings as the main distribution channel, (c) payroll/accountant partners, and (d) vertical focus (7shifts = restaurants) plus multi-unit/franchise deals to move upmarket. Referral and affiliate programs exist but are secondary.

### Inferences
- The employee app is the viral loop: every schedule published sends invites to staff, who later become managers or owners elsewhere. Large rating counts (Homebase ~96k, Connecteam ~61k) reflect employee installs, not paying accounts.
- POS integration is the most important distribution asset in hospitality: 7shifts reports more than half of its POS-integrated restaurants run Toast, and POS vendors are now both a channel and a competitor (Sling, Toast IQ Team, Square Shifts bundled). An entrant must integrate with whatever POS its target segment uses before it can be listed or recommended.
- Accountants and payroll bureaus are the main channel for payroll products (Gusto's 23,000 firms). In a market where national payroll vendors don't serve Puerto Rico, local payroll bureaus and CPA firms are a potential channel partner rather than a competitor.
- Coffee chains do buy via "preferred vendor" deals at the franchisor level (Scooter's Coffee and 7shifts), so one logo with a franchisor can deliver many locations.

### Gaps
- No vendor discloses channel mix (share of new customers from POS marketplaces, accountants, referrals or paid search).
- Reward amounts for Homebase, Gusto and Connecteam referral programs were not found.
- Puerto Rico POS market shares (Toast vs Square vs Clover vs local) not researched.

## 5. SMB SaaS benchmarks a new entrant should plan against

### Takeaway
At SMB price points (tens to low hundreds of dollars a month), plan for 3-8% monthly logo churn, net revenue retention around 90-100% (110% is "great"), 75%+ gross margin, CAC payback around 11-16 months for sub-$5k ACV, and free-to-paid conversion of ~5-9% for no-card trials or freemium versus ~30% for card-required trials. Very low ARPA (<$10/month) products have structurally bad retention.

### Cited Findings

**Growth, retention, margin, payback**
- KeyBanc/Sapphire 2025 private SaaS survey: ARR growth projected to rise from 15% (2024) to 20% (2025); gross retention "near 90%", net retention ~101% and expected to stay flat; AE payback expected to shorten to 18 months by 2026; EBITDA margins expected to turn positive by 2026 — [Sapphire 2025 survey page](https://info.sapphireventures.com/2025-keybanc-capital-markets-sapphire-ventures-saas-survey); [Barchart press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/36109511/private-saas-company-survey-reveals-ai-driven-transformation-and-sustained-operational-excellence)
- CAC payback: KeyBanc 2025 median ~20 months (secondary attribution); Benchmarkit 2025 median 16 months, and 11 months for companies selling below $5,000 ACV — [Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro-marketing.pages.dev/blog/saas-marketing-benchmarks-2026/); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/saas-marketing-statistics-2026-data-points-trends?es_p=4880540)
- Aggregated 2026 benchmarks: SMB NRR ~100%; SMB annual account churn ~15%; median B2B SaaS gross margin 75% — [SaaSRise 2026 benchmark report](https://saasrise.com/blog/saas-benchmark-report-2026) (aggregator; cites KeyBanc 2024 among sources)
- Lenny Rachitsky: for land-and-expand SMB/mid-market SaaS, ~90% NRR is "good" and ~110% "great"; user retention ~60% good, ~80% great — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29)

**Retention by ARPA (ChartMogul SaaS Retention Report, Nov 2023, ~2,100 companies, 2022 data)**
- ARPA <$10/month: top-quartile NRR 65.1%; only 2.7% of businesses exceed 100% NRR; top-quartile customer retention 63.1% (best-in-class >74.9%)
- ARPA >$500/month: top-quartile NRR 109.3%; 41.1% exceed 100% NRR; top-quartile customer retention 85.7% (best-in-class >93.1%)
- By ARR stage: top-quartile NRR 94% at $1-3M ARR, 99% at $3-15M, >105% at $15-30M; best-in-class customer retention ~85-87% at any stage — [ChartMogul SaaS Retention Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-report/)

**Monthly churn (lower-quality sources, conflicting)**
- ChartMogul analysis (reposted, unconfirmed at source): sub-$25 ARPA gross monthly MRR churn ~8.2% (6% net) vs ~2.4% for $500+ ARPA — [mowt](https://mowt.pages.dev/blog/saas-churn-rate-calculation-benchmarks)
- Other 2025-26 secondary figures: SMB monthly logo churn median ~4.7%; 3-7% typical range; Focus Digital 6.4% monthly — [UserJot](https://userjot.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-benchmarks); [Vena](https://venasolutions.com/blog/saas-churn-rate); [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/?p=6073)

**Trial and freemium conversion**
- ChartMogul/Kyle Poyar "SaaS Conversion Report" (18 Mar 2026; 200 B2B products surveyed Jan 2026; typical respondent $1-10M ARR and $50-$249 ARPA): median free-to-paid 8%; card-required trials convert at 30% (>5x no-card trials); per 1,000 visitors: freemium 90 signups → 5 paying, no-card trial 45 → 4, card-required trial 35 → 11; 62% use 14-day trials; 20% require a card — [ChartMogul Conversion Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report-2/)
- First Page Sage (via secondary): freemium 2.6%, opt-in trial 18.2%, opt-out (card) trial 48.8%; OpenView median ~14% trial vs ~7% freemium — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/saas-free-trial-vs-freemium); [Ada Chen Rekhi](https://medium.com/ada-chen-rekhi/freemium-and-free-trial-conversion-benchmarks-9fab38d096da)

**ARPA reference points for SMB workforce software (from section 1 and 4)**
- List-price ARPA for a 1-3 location hourly business ranges ~$30-$300/month before payroll; payroll adds ~$49 + $6/employee — section 1 sources
- ChartMogul's 2026 conversion respondents typically sit at $50-$249 ARPA, the band most comparable to multi-location scheduling — [ChartMogul Conversion Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report-2/)

### Inferences
- A planning model for a Puerto Rico multi-location entrant at ~$150-$300/month ARPA: 2-4% monthly logo churn if sold with onboarding to multi-unit operators (better than single-site SMB averages), NRR ~95-105% driven by location adds and payroll-export/tip add-ons, gross margin target ≥75% (the one-database-per-customer architecture must be costed against this), CAC payback ≤12 months, which with founder-led sales implies CAC under ~$2,000-$3,500 per account.
- ARPA <$25/month (single-site, per-user pricing) is where churn is worst (8%/month gross); avoid pricing that drops small chains into that band.
- Card-required or paid-pilot onboarding converts ~3-5x better than no-card trials, at the cost of fewer signups — sensible when sales are founder-led and each tenant has real infrastructure cost.

### Gaps
- No credible benchmark specific to SMB workforce/HR software (CAC, churn, NRR) was found; the numbers above are cross-industry.
- KeyBanc 2025 SMB/ACV segment cuts sit behind the report download; the 20-month CAC payback figure is a secondary attribution.
- OpenView stopped publishing after 2023; Bessemer State of the Cloud and Paddle/ProfitWell primary data were not retrieved.

## 6. What explains Homebase, 7shifts, Connecteam and Deputy scaling, and what went wrong in the category

Each scaled on one sharp wedge plus a distribution engine: Homebase on a free single-location plan plus POS integrations, then payroll; 7shifts on restaurant-only depth plus POS (Toast/Lightspeed/Square) integration, then payroll, tips and franchise deals; Connecteam on an all-in-one deskless app with a generous free tier and modular hub pricing; Deputy on partner ecosystems and international reach, then a strategic channel investor. ## 7. Implications for a one-customer entrant (multi-location hourly, first customer a Puerto Rico drive-through coffee chain)

### Takeaway
The realistic path is a vertical-plus-geography wedge (Puerto Rico multi-location food and beverage, Spanish-first, Ley 379 compliance), sold founder-led to multi-unit operators with a per-location price, a paid pilot or card-required trial instead of freemium, and payroll handled by exporting to local Puerto Rico payroll providers and accountants who become the referral channel. Expansion revenue should come from compliance, tips and payroll-export add-ons before any money movement.

### Cited Findings
- National payroll leaders exclude Puerto Rico (Gusto help center; Homebase payroll for 50 states and DC; 7shifts payroll "US-only"), leaving the payroll-attach revenue line in Puerto Rico to local providers — [Oct brief](https://support.gusto.com/article/210728164125837); [noon.ai on 7shifts payroll](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/452-7shifts-pricing-2026)
- Market price anchors for multi-location hourly businesses: $30-$150 per location per month for scheduling/time; payroll ~$39-$49 base + $6 per employee; tips $25-$50 per location — section 1 sources
- Coffee drive-through franchisors do adopt preferred workforce vendors (Scooter's Coffee and 7shifts) — [7shifts PR Mar 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/7shifts-accelerates-growth-and-industry-leadership-with-major-customer-expansions-product-innovation-and-workplace-recognition-302704760.html)
- Accountant channel economics: Gusto pays up to 20% revenue share and counts only clients added through the partner dashboard — [Gusto partner program](https://gusto.com/partner-resources/gusto-partner-program-for-accountants)
- Card-required trials convert ~30% vs ~8% median free-to-paid — [ChartMogul Conversion Report 2026](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report-2/)
- ARPA below $10-$25/month carries the worst retention (top-quartile NRR 65%; gross monthly churn ~8%) — [ChartMogul Retention Report 2023](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-report/); [mowt](https://mowt.pages.dev/blog/saas-churn-rate-calculation-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- **Business-model gaps visible against leaders:** (1) no live billing or self-serve plans, so no price signal or trial funnel; (2) no published price or packaging; (3) no expansion line (payroll, tips, hiring, compliance packs, AI); (4) no distribution partner (POS, payroll bureau, accountant, franchisor); (5) no referral or affiliate mechanism; (6) one-database-per-customer raises cost per tenant, which makes a freemium tier expensive.
- **Wedge:** "Puerto Rico multi-location food and beverage operations": Spanish-first, Ley 379 overtime and meal rules, payroll-ready exports for PR providers, punch receipts. No national player covers this combination (Oct brief). Expand later to other PR hourly verticals (retail, pharmacies, gas stations) and then Spanish-speaking US markets.
- **Pricing model:** per location, with a per-active-employee component above a threshold, or a flat bundle for small chains (e.g., 1-5 locations). Benchmarks suggest ~$60-$150 per location per month for scheduling, time, messaging and PR compliance; a "Compliance Pack" or Premium tier at the upper end; tips and payroll-export at $20-$50 per location. Offer 15-20% annual prepay (industry norm) and a monthly minimum (~$30-$50, like Deputy). Keep pricing transparent with clear tier limits, given the category's billing-surprise complaints. Treat these numbers as hypotheses to test with the first customer.
- **Trial model:** a paid pilot or card-required 14-30-day trial with founder-led onboarding, not freemium. A free tier only makes sense later with shared infrastructure for small tenants.
- **Channel order of realism:** (1) founder-led direct sales to PR multi-unit operators, using the first chain as reference; (2) PR accountants and payroll bureaus as referral partners with a revenue share (Gusto-style 10-20%), since they can't get PR payroll from national tools; (3) POS marketplace listings for whatever POS the PR coffee/QSR segment uses (verify Toast, Square, Clover presence in PR); (4) franchisor preferred-vendor deals; (5) a customer referral credit (one reward per company, on paid conversion, as Homebase does). App-store presence matters for employee adoption (Oct brief).
- **Expansion sequence:** PR compliance pack → tip management/pooling → payroll export plus a partner revenue share with PR payroll providers → hiring/onboarding in Spanish → AI payroll-readiness checks (included, as Deputy does) → only later, if at all, EWA through a licensed partner.
- **Metrics to plan against:** 2-4% monthly logo churn target, NRR ≥100% via location adds, gross margin ≥75% after per-tenant database cost, CAC payback ≤12 months.

### Gaps
- Puerto Rico market size (number of multi-location hourly employers, coffee/QSR chains, POS share) was not researched here.
- No benchmark found on what PR payroll bureaus or CPAs would accept as a referral share.
- Willingness to pay of Puerto Rico operators is unknown; prices above are extrapolated from US list prices.
