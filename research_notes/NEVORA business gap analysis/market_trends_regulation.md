# Market size, labor regulation, technology shifts and expansion paths for a Puerto Rico-born hourly-workforce app (as of November 2026)

*Scope note: extends `docs/product/trends-2026-10.md` (UX and platform patterns: passkeys, push prompts, text size, offline, AI finder) with market, regulatory and go-to-market evidence. Competitor features and pricing are covered by other researchers and are excluded. Research date 2026-11-18. Data years are labelled; where sources conflict, both are given.*

## 1. Market size and growth (WFM software, US hourly workers, small hourly employers, Puerto Rico, Hispanic-owned businesses, bilingual demand)

### Takeaway
Workforce-management (WFM) software is roughly a $7–12B global market in 2025, growing at about 4–13% a year depending on the analyst, and analysts disagree heavily on scope. The addressable labor pool is large and stable: 81.5M US hourly workers in 2025, a restaurant industry of 15.8M jobs, and 845k franchise units. Puerto Rico is a small but growing beachhead: about 41k small employers, and food-service jobs that reached a record 78.8k in 2025, up 27% from 2015. The US Hispanic-owned business segment (about 496k employer firms with $730B in receipts, 2023 data) is the natural next market for a bilingual product.

### Inferences
- **Puerto Rico is a beachhead, not a business on its own.** Puerto Rico has about 5.3–5.7k food-service establishments plus retail. At an illustrative $100 per location per month (an assumption; pricing is out of scope), 100% penetration of PR accommodation and food service would be about $6.9M ARR. Growth requires the mainland or other verticals.
- Analyst growth rates (4–13%) matter less than the shift inside the market: owner-run multi-location hourly businesses are moving from paper and texts to apps. The second-order signals are the fastest-growing formats (drive-through coffee +87.5% units at 7 Brew; franchise +1.5% units) and the 81.5M hourly workers.
- Bilingual is a differentiator in purchase behaviour (Verizon's 77% vs 69%), but the evidence that owners *require* Spanish admin UIs is thin. The value is probably bilingual employee-facing surfaces plus Spanish-capable support, not translation alone.
- The PR food-service labour pool hit a record in 2025 while the minimum wage rose from $8.50 to $10.50 between 2023 and 2024 (see section 2). That raised the wage floor about 24% in roughly 18 months, which strengthens the case for labour-cost tooling in PR.

### Gaps
- No analyst sizes the "SMB hourly WFM" segment (Homebase/7shifts/Connecteam tier) separately from enterprise WFM; I found no reliable SMB-only figure.
- 2025 BLS share of hourly workers among all wage earners was not in the table fetched; 2024's 55.6% is the latest confirmed.
- Number of US *employer* firms in hourly-heavy industries (restaurants, retail, home care) under 50 employees was not retrieved from Census SUSB.
- PR 2023–2024 County Business Patterns food-service establishment counts were not available in the sources reached; PR retail establishment counts not retrieved.
- FL/TX Hispanic-owned employer firm counts: a secondary summary gives ~86k (18.1%) in Florida and ~63.6k (14.6%) in Texas, but I could not tie the figures to a Census year or confirm they cover employer firms only. Verify against the Census ABS state tables.
- No 2025–2026 survey specifically measures demand for Spanish-language back-office software. A circulating "62% of Hispanic-owned businesses have Spanish-preferring back-office staff" claim is unverified.

## 2. Puerto Rico labor law the software must encode

### Takeaway
Puerto Rico has the most complex hour-based rules of any US jurisdiction relevant here. Daily overtime starts after 8 hours in a **calendar day**, on top of weekly overtime after 40. A meal-period window and penalty apply, plus a seventh-day rest premium, accrual thresholds of 130 hours per month, and a Christmas bonus with its own hours year (Oct 1–Sep 30). **Many rates depend on whether the employee was hired before or after January 26, 2017.** The 2022 reform (Act 41-2022) was voided in March 2023, so Act 4-2017 rules apply. The minimum wage has been $10.50 since July 1, 2024, and I found no official 2026 increase. The 2025 legislation that affects time tracking is the paid-lactation-break requirement (Acts 29-2025 and 87-2025).

### Cited Findings
**Status of the statutes**
- Act 4-2017, the Labor Transformation and Flexibility Act (LTFA), was approved January 26, 2017 and took effect immediately. Many provisions apply differently to employees hired before and after that date — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws). Statute text: [Act 4-2017 (OGP)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2-ingles/0004-2017.pdf), [Act 379-1948 (OGP)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2-ingles/379-1948.pdf)
- Act 41-2022 (signed June 20, 2022) was declared null and void ab initio on March 3, 2023 by Judge Laura Taylor Swain in the PROMESA Title III court, which permanently enjoined its enforcement — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/federal-court-strikes-down-amendments-puerto-rico-2017-employment-law-reform). Prior Act 4-2017 entitlements were reinstated — [Ogletree, Mar 9, 2023](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/employee-benefits-and-rights-in-puerto-rico-what-to-know-now-that-law-41-2022-has-been-declared-null-and-void/). A 2023 practice guide notes that the Oversight Board's position left Act 41's enforceability "in question" before the ruling — [Chambers 2023](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/employment-2023/puerto-rico). I did not check for later appeals.

**Hours and overtime**
- The regular workday is 8 hours and the regular workweek 40 hours. Act 4-2017 replaced the rolling "any 24-hour period" overtime trigger with the **calendar day**, removing "technical" overtime from shifting schedules — [Morgan Lewis 2022 summary](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/06/puerto-rico-amends-2017-labor-reform); [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws). The workweek is 168 consecutive hours (per a search summary of the statute; verify in Act 379 §).
- Overtime over 8 hours in a calendar day or 40 in a week is generally paid at 1.5× the regular rate. Secretary of Labor Opinion of **Sept 13, 2024** says employers may require overtime with no limit other than paying the premium, absent health and safety risks — [Jackson Lewis](https://jacksonlewis.com/insights/what-puerto-rico-labor-secretarys-new-overtime-interpretation-means-employers). **Conflict:** a 2023 practice guide says overtime is "time and a half or double the agreed rate", and blogs say 2× may still apply to some pre-2017 hires. Confirm in Act 379 as amended — [Chambers 2023](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/employment-2023/puerto-rico)
- Alternative workweek: by written agreement, up to 10 hours a day (for example 4×10) without daily overtime, if the week stays at or under 40. Employees may make up time missed for personal reasons. Act 4-2017 created a procedure for employees to request changes to shifts, hours or location, with employer response deadlines — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/06/puerto-rico-amends-2017-labor-reform)
- Hours on the weekly day of rest count as overtime (Act 379 Art. 4(d)) — [Littler PRDOL Guidelines ch. 4, 5, 7](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/guidelines-interpretation-puerto-ricos-employment-legislation-chapters-4-5-and-7)

**Day of rest (Act 289-1946)**
- Every non-exempt employee gets one day of rest for every six days worked. Work on the rest day is paid at **1.5× for employees hired on or after Jan 26, 2017** and **2× for employees hired earlier**. Each week is an independent unit, so the premium does not extend past the seventh day — [Littler Guidelines](https://www.littler.com/publication-press/publication/guidelines-interpretation-puerto-ricos-employment-legislation-0); [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/puerto-rico-update-new-employment-benefits-approved.html). Student employees get 2× unless they work for micro, small or medium businesses, which may pay 1.5× — [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/puerto-rico-update-new-employment-benefits-approved.html). Statute: [Act 289-1946](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/2-ingles/289-1946.pdf)

**Meal periods**
- The meal period starts no earlier than the end of the 2nd hour of work and before the start of the 6th consecutive hour. It lasts one hour and can be reduced to 30 minutes by written agreement; after the first year either party may revoke the reduction — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/employee-benefits-and-rights-in-puerto-rico-what-to-know-now-that-law-41-2022-has-been-declared-null-and-void/)
- Penalty for work during the meal period: for post-Jan 26, 2017 hires, the regular rate plus half the regular rate for the meal period or fraction worked (effectively 1.5×). Pre-2017 hires keep the older, higher penalty (historically double) — [Littler on the 2017 bill (Mondaq)](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/employee-benefits-compensation/1204686/hot-off-the-press-new-labor-reform-bill-for-puerto-rico); [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/amendments-puerto-rico-2017-employment-law-reform-employers-need-know)
- Second meal period: not required when the workday is 12 hours or less and the first meal was taken. Act 4-2017 removed the written-agreement requirement for this waiver — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws); [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/amendments-puerto-rico-2017-employment-law-reform-employers-need-know)
- **Conflict:** whether the first meal period may be waived when the day is 6 hours or less. A 2023 guide says yes — [Chambers 2023](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/employment-2023/puerto-rico); other summaries differ.

**Vacation, sick leave and severance (restored Act 4-2017 rules)**
- Non-exempt employees accrue leave only in months with **at least 130 hours worked** (Act 41-2022 had lowered this to 115; the 115 threshold was voided) — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/employee-benefits-and-rights-in-puerto-rico-what-to-know-now-that-law-41-2022-has-been-declared-null-and-void/); [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws)
- Vacation for post-2017 hires: employers with 12 or fewer employees, ½ day per month. Employers with 13 or more: ½ day per month up to 1 year of service, ¾ day for 1–5 years, 1 day for 5–15 years, 1¼ days for 15+ years. Sick leave: 1 day per month for any employer size. Part-time non-exempt employees get no statutory accrual. Employees may cash out only vacation above 10 days — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/employee-benefits-and-rights-in-puerto-rico-what-to-know-now-that-law-41-2022-has-been-declared-null-and-void/)
- Probation: 270 days for non-exempt staff, 360 days for exempt. Statute of limitations for contract, vacation, sick leave and unjust dismissal claims: 1 year. Severance (Act 80) for employees hired after Jan 26, 2017 is capped at 9 months' pay — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/employee-benefits-and-rights-in-puerto-rico-what-to-know-now-that-law-41-2022-has-been-declared-null-and-void/)

**Christmas bonus (bono de Navidad, Law 148-1969)**
- Paid between **Nov 15 and Dec 15**. The hours-count year runs Oct 1–Sep 30. Late payment adds a penalty of 50% of the bonus if paid within 6 months and 100% after that — [Jackson Lewis, Nov 12, 2025](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/avoiding-naughty-list-christmas-bonus-compliance-puerto-rico-employers)
- Pre-Jan 26, 2017 hires: eligible after **700 hours**. Employers with 15 or fewer employees pay 3% of the first $10,000 (max $300); employers with more than 15 pay 6% of the first $10,000 (max $600) — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/avoiding-naughty-list-christmas-bonus-compliance-puerto-rico-employers)
- Post-2017 hires: eligible after **1,350 hours**; 2% of wages earned in the period, up to $600. Jackson Lewis also gives a $300 cap for employers with 20 or fewer employees, which conflicts internally. The first-year maximum is 50% of the normal amount — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/avoiding-naughty-list-christmas-bonus-compliance-puerto-rico-employers)
- Exemption for insufficient profits: notify PR-DOL by **Dec 1** with a CPA-certified balance sheet and P&L for Oct 1–Sep 30 — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/avoiding-naughty-list-christmas-bonus-compliance-puerto-rico-employers). Other bonuses paid during the year may be credited against it with written notice — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws)

**Minimum wage**
- **$10.50 an hour since July 1, 2024** (up from $9.50), an automatic step under Act 47-2021 unless the Minimum Wage Review Commission decided otherwise — [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/puerto-rico-increases-hourly-minimum-9772944); [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/node/29852). FRED lists $10.50 for 2024, 2025 and 2026 (and $8.50 at Jan 1, 2023) — [FRED STTMINWGPR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/STTMINWGPR). Jackson Lewis says it is uncertain if or when the next increase will come — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/node/29852). The DTRH commission report: [trabajo.pr.gov](https://trabajo.pr.gov/docs/Avisos/General%20Minimum%20Wage%20Report.pdf) (not retrieved)

**Minors (Act 230-1942, Ley de Empleo de Menores)**
- The DTRH administers work permits for minors, with employer declaration and parent certification forms — [DTRH Ley 230](https://trabajo.pr.gov/docs/Normas_del_Trabajo/Leyes/Ley_Num_230_12_mayo_1942_Empleo%20de%20Menores.pdf); [DTRH permit information](https://trabajo.pr.gov/docs/Normas_del_Trabajo/Menores/Informaci%C3%B3n%20General%20-%20Permisos%20de%20Menores.pdf)
- Secondary sources: a permit is required under 18. Ages 16–17 may work 8 hours a day, 40 a week and 6 days a week, but not between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. — [minimum-wage.org](https://www.minimum-wage.org/puerto-rico/child-labor-laws). **Conflict:** another secondary source gives 6 hours on school days (see Gaps)

**Recordkeeping and electronic documents**
- Act 4-2017: electronic signatures and notices have the same effect as written ones — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-approves-major-reform-its-employment-laws)
- PR wage statements (499R-2/W-2PR) must be filed electronically through Hacienda's SURI portal in EFW2PR format — [Hacienda Pub. 19-01](https://hacienda.pr.gov/sites/default/files/publicaciones/2019/10/publication_19-01.pdf). 2025 specs were published in Nov 2025 — [Mercans](https://mercans.com/resources/statutory-alerts/puerto-rico-2025-w2pr-electronic-filing-specs-publication-2501); 2026 specs — [Hacienda Pub. 26-01](https://hacienda.pr.gov/sites/default/files/publicaciones/2026/10/publication_26-01.pdf). PR employers also make local disability (SINOT) and PR unemployment contributions — [TopSource](https://topsourceworldwide.com/?p=12015)

**2024–2026 changes**
- Sept 13, 2024 overtime opinion (above).
- **Act 29-2025** (signed June 23, 2025) amends Act 427-2000: employers must provide a private space for milk expression, lactation breaks are paid, and discipline over them is prohibited — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/puerto-rico-new-act-29-2025-essential-employer-obligations-breastfeedingpumping-activities); [Reichard & Escalera](https://reichardescalera.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Labor-UpdatePuerto-Rico-Enacts-Act-29-2025-Expanded-Protections-for-Nursing-Employees.pdf). **Act 87-2025** (Aug 1, 2025) consolidates lactation laws into a Lactation Code, enforceable by the Women's Advocate Office and DTRH with fines — [McConnell Valdés](https://www.mcvpr.com/newsroom/publications/PR-Lactation-Code)
- Act 37-2025 (July 3, 2025) on arbitration of private employment disputes, and Act 26-2025 (June 12, 2025) on veterans' hiring preferences — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/puerto-rico-governor-signs-two-new-acts-addressing-arbitration-and-preferences)

### Inferences
- **The hire-date cohort must be a first-class employee attribute.** One Jan 26, 2017 flag changes five calculations: rest-day rate (2× or 1.5×), meal penalty (2× or 1.5×), Christmas bonus threshold and formula (700 or 1,350 hours), vacation accrual table, and severance cap. A coffee chain founded after 2017 may have no pre-2017 staff, but acquired locations or franchise conversions will.
- A trustworthy PR timecard engine needs:
  - calendar-day and weekly overtime that does not double count
  - a meal-window detector: no punch-out between hour 2 and hour 6 means a penalty, with a 30-minute written-agreement flag
  - seventh-consecutive-day detection
  - a monthly 130-hour accrual gate
  - an Oct–Sep Christmas bonus hours ledger with a Nov 15–Dec 15 payment reminder and a Dec 1 exemption deadline reminder
  - minors' hour and time-of-day blocks in the scheduler
  - paid lactation breaks as a distinct paid break type
- The product only shows payroll amounts calculated elsewhere, so it can turn these rules into **hours classification and alerts** (for example, "Meal penalty: 0.5 h at 1.5× owed") without computing tax. That makes it a strong partner for PR payroll bureaus.
- The one-year statute of limitations is shorter than the FLSA's, but federal retention (3 years for payroll, 2 for time cards) still governs storage.

### Gaps

It conflicts with the 8/40/6 figure, and I could not pin it to a specific URL or to 29 LPRA §433. Verify with DTRH Normas del Trabajo.

## 3. US federal and state rules (FLSA, daily overtime, fair workweek, breaks, EWA, biometrics, recordkeeping, new tax reporting)

### Takeaway
Federal rules were stable in 2026. The salary threshold went back to $684 a week after the 2024 rule died, and the main federal novelty is **2026 W-2 reporting of "qualified overtime" (Box 12, Code TT)** under the 2025 tax law. Complexity sits at state and city level: four states with daily overtime (plus PR), 14 states with meal-break statutes, Oregon plus about 10 cities with fair-workweek laws, about a dozen states regulating earned wage access, and BIPA-style biometric rules. Earned wage access became federally lighter in Dec 2025 but state enforcement increased.

### Cited Findings
**FLSA**
- The DOL rescinded the 2024 overtime rule (around May 13–15, 2026) after dropping its appeal on May 5, 2026, restoring the 2019 thresholds: **$684 a week ($35,568 a year)** and $107,432 for highly compensated employees. Duties tests still apply and higher state thresholds still govern — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/status-quo-dol-final-rule-keeps-overtime-salary-levels-2019-thresholds); [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/employment-law/labor-department-kills-2024-flsa-overtime-rule-restores-2019-salary-thresholds/575650). The Restoring Overtime Pay Act of 2026 (S. 4551, which would set $45,000) is only proposed — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/employee-rights-labour-relations/1801958/2026-changes-to-flsa-overtime-exemption-rules-what-employers-should-know-about-revised-salary-requirements)
- Recordkeeping: payroll records kept at least **3 years** (29 CFR 516.5). Time cards, wage-rate tables, work schedules and records of additions to or deductions from wages kept at least **2 years** (29 CFR 516.6). Employers may use any timekeeping method — [DOL WHD Fact Sheet 21](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping); [29 CFR 516.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/516.6). Electronic records are allowed if they are viewable and identifiable by date or pay period — [K-State summary of DOL rules](https://www.k-state.edu/hr/docs/FLSARecordkeeping.pdf). A vendor's "72-hour retrieval" claim is unverified — [Clockspot](https://www.clockspot.com/research/recordkeeping-requirements-by-state)

**"No tax on overtime" (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, July 2025)**
- Employers must report qualified overtime on the **2026 Form W-2 in Box 12, Code TT**. For 2025 separate reporting was not required and the IRS waived penalties. Employees can generally claim only what is reported, so errors need a W-2c — [Netchex](https://netchex.com/blog/no-tax-on-overtime-in-2026-payroll-w-2-reporting-guide-for-employers/); [IRS FS-2026-13](https://www.irs.gov/pub/taxpros/fs-2026-13.pdf); [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/print/v2/content/1573946/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-employer-reporting-obligations-for-qualified-overtime-compensation.pdf)
- Vendors warn that daily-overtime jurisdictions (California, PR) need federal FLSA overtime tracked separately from local daily overtime, because only FLSA-required overtime qualifies — [TimeTrex](https://www.timetrex.com/blog/no-tax-on-overtime-2026)

**Daily overtime and breaks**
- Daily overtime: California (1.5× after 8 hours, 2× after 12, seventh-consecutive-day premium, Lab. Code §510), Alaska (after 8), Nevada (after 8, only for workers under a wage cutoff; **conflict:** sources give $18/h or 1.5× the state minimum), Colorado (after 12) — [Clockspot overtime by state](https://www.clockspot.com/research/overtime-laws-by-state); [beancount guide, Oct 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/10/05/daily-overtime-double-time-california-alaska-nevada-employer-guide)
- 14 states have statewide meal-break statutes (CA, CO, CT, DE, IL, KY, MA, MN from Jan 1, 2026, NV, NY, OR, RI, TN, WA) and 7 have rest-break statutes (CA, CO, KY, MN from Jan 1, 2026, NV, OR, WA). California's premium is 1 hour of pay per missed meal plus 1 per missed rest, at most 2 a day (Lab. Code §226.7) — [Clockspot meal and rest breaks](https://www.clockspot.com/research/meal-and-rest-break-laws-by-state)

**Predictive scheduling / fair workweek**
- Oregon is the only statewide law (retail, hospitality and food service employers with 500+ employees worldwide). Cities: NYC, San Francisco, Seattle, Philadelphia, Chicago, Los Angeles City, **LA County (effective July 1, 2025)**, Berkeley, Emeryville, Evanston — [Homebase 2026 guide](https://joinhomebase.com/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws/); [Deel](https://www.deel.com/blog/fair-workweek-laws-by-state/). Counts differ between "Oregon + 10" and "11 jurisdictions" — [WorkAxle](https://www.workaxle.com/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws-2026)
- 14-day advance posting is the norm. NYC fast food covers chains with 30+ locations nationally — [Deel](https://www.deel.com/blog/fair-workweek-laws-by-state/). Chicago pays 50% of wages for shifts cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice — [Nory 2026 guide](https://www.nory.ai/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws). A "$10–$75 per change" NYC predictability-pay range from a vendor is unverified.
- Several states preempt local scheduling laws (one partial list: AL, AR, **FL**, GA, IN, IA, KS, MI, OH, TN …). Nine states had bills under consideration (CT, HI, IL, MA, MN, NJ, NC, RI, WV) — [Homebase](https://joinhomebase.com/blog/predictive-scheduling-laws/)
- Evidence: fair-workweek laws raised the share of workers with at least 2 weeks' notice by 13 percentage points — [Harvard Shift fact sheet, July 2026](https://shift.hks.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/fww_factsheet_report_final.pdf). Upjohn found no employment effect from NYC's fast-food law — [Upjohn](https://www.upjohn.org/research-highlights/do-fair-workweek-laws-affect-labor-markets)

**Earned wage access (EWA)**
- The CFPB rescinded the 2020 Payactiv advisory opinion in **Jan 2025** — [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2025/01/cfpb-rescinds-earned-wage-access-advisory-opinion/). In **Dec 2025** it issued a new advisory opinion that "covered", employer-facilitated EWA is **not credit** under TILA and Reg Z, and withdrew the 2024 proposed interpretive rule. The opinion is non-binding — [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260107-cfpb-reestablishes-position-that-certain-earned-wage)
- The safe harbor requires advances based on **payroll data** (not worker-estimated hours), repayment by **payroll deduction** (not a bank debit after payday), and no recourse — [Goodwin, Mar 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/insights-finance-cfs-yir-payday-and-small-dollar-lending)
- States: about a dozen states have EWA laws as of 2026 (industry guide). MD, AR, MO and NV treat some EWA as loans, Arkansas and Utah regulate providers, and California regulates through its consumer financial protection law. Bills are pending in NY, TX and GA — [Capstone](https://capstonedc.com/?p=54499); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/insights-finance-cfs-yir-payday-and-small-dollar-lending). The NY AG sued two providers in Apr 2025, alleging 350–750% APRs, and Baltimore sued MoneyLion in Oct 2025 — [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/insights-finance-cfs-yir-payday-and-small-dollar-lending)
- Economics: South Carolina's 2025 report shows an average advance of about $103 and per-transaction fees of $1.50–$9.74 — [SC Consumer Affairs](https://www.consumer.sc.gov/sites/consumer/files/Documents/Reports/2025%20EWA%20Report%20Draft%20FINAL.pdf). In CFPB data (2021–22) the average employer-partnered advance was about $106, fees were $0.61–$4.70, about 90% of users paid at least one fee, and the illustrative APR was 109.5% — [CFPB Data Spotlight](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/data-spotlight-developments-in-the-paycheck-advance-market/)

**Biometric time clocks**
- Illinois BIPA's 2024 amendment limits damages to one recovery per person, not per scan, and the 7th Circuit held on **Apr 1, 2026** that it applies retroactively to pending cases (the case involved fingerprint time clocks). Notice and written-consent duties are unchanged — [Paul Hastings](https://paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/7th-circuit-confirms-bipa-amendment-has-retroactive-application); [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/employment-law/seventh-circuit-limits-bipa-damages-to-one-recovery-per-employee/570970)
- A bill to exempt biometric time clocks that store only templates (SB3122) was referred to committee on Feb 2, 2026 and has not been enacted — [ILGA SB3122](https://my.ilga.gov/ftp/legislation/104/BillStatus/HTML/10400SB3122.html)
- The Colorado Privacy Act biometric amendment (C.R.S. §6-1-1314) took effect July 1, 2025. Texas and Washington have standalone biometric statutes requiring notice and consent — [BCLP biometric tracker](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/insights/us-biometric-laws-and-pending-legislation-tracker.html)

### Inferences
- **Jurisdiction rule packs, not hard-coded rules.** The engine should parameterise: daily-overtime thresholds by jurisdiction (PR 8, CA 8/12 plus seventh day, AK 8, NV 8 with a wage condition, CO 12), meal and rest windows with premiums, seventh-day rules, scheduling-notice windows with premium reasons, and minors' limits. Separating FLSA overtime from local daily overtime is now also needed for Code TT reporting.
- **Fair-workweek exposure is low on the likely expansion path.** Florida preempts local scheduling laws and Texas has no such ordinances in the sources found. Advance-notice logging and change-reason capture are still a cheap trust feature and become required in NYC, Chicago, Seattle, Philadelphia and Oregon.
- **EWA is an opportunity only with payroll integration.** The CFPB safe harbor requires payroll-data-based advances repaid by payroll deduction. A product that shows externally calculated pay statements cannot offer covered EWA alone; it would need an embedded-payroll or EWA partner that can deduct, and a state-by-state licence check.
- The October report's choice to keep the kiosk on PIN and avoid face recognition is legally reinforced: biometric clock-in would carry BIPA-style notice and consent duties in IL, TX, WA and CO. Passkeys with on-device Face ID keep biometrics on the phone. Whether this falls outside BIPA was not confirmed; treat it as an inference.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved on the federal ESIGN Act or UETA as applied to timecard and schedule acknowledgements; only PR's Act 4-2017 e-signature provision was confirmed.
- Exact predictability-pay amounts for NYC, Seattle, SF, Philadelphia and LA in 2026 were not confirmed from official city sources.
- Complete list and effective dates of state EWA statutes (2025–2026) not compiled from primary sources; the "about a dozen" count comes from an industry guide.
- Texas biometric statute (CUBI) details and any 2025–2026 Texas changes were not retrieved.
- Whether "qualified overtime" deductions matter for PR-source wages (PR residents are generally taxed by Hacienda on PR-source income) was not confirmed.

## 4. Technology and buyer-behavior shifts, 2025–2026

### Takeaway
AI in hourly-workforce operations moved from curiosity to mainstream in 2026: about 39% of restaurants use some AI, and scheduling and labour management is the top use. The proven value is labour-cost reduction from forecasting and scheduling, not chat. Embedded-payroll APIs (Check, Gusto Embedded) now let small vertical vendors offer payroll, but I found **no evidence they support Puerto Rico payroll tax**. Frontline staff still run on personal messaging apps. Buyers are owner-led and cost-sensitive, and 59% regret at least one software purchase. Implementation failure is the main regret driver, which favours vendors who do onboarding for the customer.

### Inferences
- **The AI owner advisor should talk in labour-cost dollars.** The buyer-visible AI win is "labour as % of sales" and overtime avoided, using POS sales and scheduled hours. Restaurant365's 62% figure and the NRA's 34% forecasting use support this. It matches the October report's "narrow, sourced answers, no open chat" stance. A PR-specific angle no mainland AI covers: forecasting PR premiums before they happen (meal penalty, seventh day, 8-hour daily overtime) and Christmas bonus accrual.
- **Embedded payroll is not an easy path in PR.** The mainland APIs advertise "all 50 states", which says nothing about territories. If Check or Gusto Embedded cannot file W-2PR/499R-2, SINOT and PR unemployment, the current model (statements with amounts calculated elsewhere) plus integrations with PR payroll bureaus is the realistic path in PR. Embedded payroll becomes a lever on the mainland.
- **Implementation is the moat for small buyers.** Regret is driven by implementation disruption, and only about a third adopt smoothly. Done-for-you setup (importing staff, schedules and PR cohort flags) and bilingual onboarding address the main cause of churn better than more features.
- Personal-messaging use for shift work (vendor-reported) means in-app messaging must beat WhatsApp on *records*: acknowledgements, schedule-change audit trails and fair-workweek evidence. Convenience alone will not win.

## 5. Expansion levers for a Puerto Rico-born product

### Takeaway
The most realistic sequence is: (1) deepen PR across adjacent hourly verticals and multi-unit and franchise operators, where PR-specific labour rules are a moat; (2) expand to US-mainland Hispanic-owner hubs, where Florida is attractive because it preempts local scheduling laws and 18% of its firms are reported Hispanic-owned, and Texas because it leads franchise growth; (3) treat Latin America, starting with the Dominican Republic, as a later bet, because its labour code (44-hour week, tiered overtime) and payroll systems differ entirely. Act 60's 4% export-services rate rewards selling outside PR, which is a financial reason to move to the mainland early.

### Inferences
- **PR moat first.** No mainland WFM vendor in the sources encodes PR's hire-date cohorts, meal window, seventh-day premium, 130-hour accrual gate and Christmas bonus ledger. "Built for PR law, in PR Spanish" is a credible wedge with PR multi-unit operators (QSR, coffee, pharmacies and retail chains, home care, security guards). It also lets the product partner with PR payroll bureaus and CPAs, which file PR payroll that mainland APIs may not.
- **Adjacent verticals ranked by fit (inference):**
  1. QSR, coffee and bakeries: same shift patterns
  2. Multi-unit retail and pharmacy
  3. Franchise operators, where one owner has many units and the product sells per location
  4. Cleaning and security guards: post-based scheduling and geofenced clock-in; needs field, not kiosk
  5. Home care: mainland Medicaid EVV requirements add a separate compliance layer, not researched here
- **Mainland path:** Florida first for Spanish-dominant owners, Florida's local scheduling-law preemption and air links to PR, then Texas for franchise growth. Package rule sets for FLSA, the four daily-overtime states and fair-workweek cities only when entering those markets.
- **Act 60 math:** export-services income (mainland and LatAm customers) is taxed at 4%; PR-customer revenue is not eligible. A PR software company has a structural tax incentive to grow mainland revenue early. Confirm decree eligibility with PR tax counsel.
