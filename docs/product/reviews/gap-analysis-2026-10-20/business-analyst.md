## Executive summary

1. The employee and Clocker experiences are close to sellable. What blocks the business is outside the screens: NEVORA runs **one company per deployment** ("SaaS is paused"), and each new company is created by an engineer running a CLI script against an empty database.
2. Pay is the biggest trust risk. Native payroll calculates Puerto Rico and federal taxes under an **owner-waived provisional policy with no accountant sign-off**. The hours export sends **total hours only**, with no regular, overtime or meal-penalty split, so the payroll provider receives hours that may be priced wrong.
3. For the first market, Spanish reaches only the sign-in pages and the Clocker. The portal hard-codes `lang="en"`, the invitation email is English-only, and the Puerto Rico rules (Ley 379 meal and overtime) still wait for labour counsel.
4. Launch basics are missing: no Terms of Service, Privacy Policy or recorded consent; no error monitoring or alerting; no billing; no help centre; and an account-deletion message that sends people to another brand's email domain.
5. Hiring a team is slow. Only the Owner can invite, by email only, and each link expires after **15 minutes** (a database constraint). A CSV staff import exists in `src/lib` but no screen or endpoint uses it. Schedule and time-off alerts go by web push only, with no SMS or email.

## Short notes

**What already works (keep it, and market it).** Append-only corrections and timecard revisions where the approver can't be the employee. Punch receipts and attendance observations. Payroll access closed to Managers by default (`configurable-payroll.md:73–74`). Deactivation that preserves history and ends sessions. Rate-limited PINs and invitations. ICS calendar feeds. Location-scoped manager authority. These are real trust advantages. Most of the gaps above surround them; they are not flaws in them.

**End-to-end flow check (hire to pay statement).**
- Hire → invite: works, with friction (row 7).
- Activate: works.
- Schedule: works.
- Clock: works online only (row 13).
- Timecard approval: works, with no employee sign-off (row 15).
- Payroll export: hours only, no OT split (row 3).
- Native payroll: provisional tax policy (row 2).
- Pay statement: works, English and Spanish PDFs per plan (`configurable-payroll.md:100–101`).
- Breaks in the chain: the export, legal sign-off, and anything before the Owner exists (row 1).

**Counterparts missing between roles.**
- Owner invites; Manager can't (row 7).
- Employee requests time off and the Manager decides, but no reason is recorded (row 14).
- Manager approves the timecard; the employee has no matching confirmation (row 15).
- Employee requests corrections; Lead has no visibility (row 19).

**Inference versus confirmed.**
- Confirmed in code or docs: rows 1–8, 10–12, 14–19 and 22.
- Partly inferred: rows 9 (statutory accrual), 14 (retention periods), 16 (future-shift behaviour not traced) and 5 (the dead "deleting" branch: no producer found by grep).

None of this is legal advice. Puerto Rico labour counsel decides on rows 3, 9, 14 and 16. A payroll CPA decides on rows 2 and 20. Privacy counsel decides on rows 5 and 21. Jose decides on rows 1, 7, 11 and 19.

**Suggested order for the next two weeks (no build approval implied).**
1. Book counsel and a CPA (rows 2, 3, 5, 9).
2. Decide pilot-versus-SaaS (row 1).
3. Fix the support domain string and add ToS and Privacy pages (row 5).
4. Add monitoring and a restore drill (row 6).

These six remove most of the launch risk without new product scope.
