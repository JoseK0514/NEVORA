# 35 PWA Engineer — installability, offline, push, updates, iOS standalone

**Verdict.** The push and service-worker code is careful about privacy. It never caches signed-in pages or data, and it asks for notification permission only when someone taps a button. The structure around it is weak, though. The service worker, the offline page, the install button and the update notice all load only when push notifications are configured. Sign-in sits outside the installed app's scope. An employee who goes offline loses the one thing they need most, their next shift. Nothing tells an installed app that a new version has shipped. Fix those four before you promote "install NEVORA" to Owners and Managers.

## Recommendations

1. **Register the service worker for everyone, separately from push.**
   *What:* Register `/fuel-portal-sw.js` from the app shell for every signed-in role. Do it once, after load, with no conditions. Keep push enrollment as a separate, opt-in step. Move the `beforeinstallprompt` capture and the "update ready" listener into the shell as well.
   *Why:* Today registration happens in only one place: `DeviceController.readStatus` → `runtime.prepare()` (`src/lib/notifications/device-client.ts:74-75, 339`). That code runs only when push `configurationAvailable` is true. Its component is mounted only when `notificationsEnabled` is true (`src/components/app-shell.tsx:446`). With push turned off or misconfigured in an environment, there is no offline page (it is served by the worker, `public/fuel-portal-sw.js:95`), no "Add to Home Screen" button and no update message. The `installPrompt: false` capability is also hard-coded (`device-client.ts:314`).
   *Impact:* High · *Effort:* S · *Owner:* UI.

2. **Keep sign-in, activation and session expiry inside the installed app.**
   *What:* Change the manifest `scope` to `/`, keep `start_url: /app`, and link the manifest and the apple-touch-icon from `/login`, `/activate`, `/forgot-access` and `/reset-access` as well. To keep the Clocker kiosk from installing as the portal, don't link the manifest from `/clocker`, and give the kiosk its own manifest if it should be installable. Then test on a real iPhone: install, sign in inside the standalone app, let the session expire, and sign in again.
   *Why:* The scope is `/app` (`src/lib/notifications/manifest.ts:17`). The manifest and the `appleWebApp` meta exist only in `src/app/app/layout.tsx:37-39`. An expired session redirects to `/login` (`src/lib/auth/session.ts:113`), which is out of scope. On iOS, a Home Screen app keeps its own cookies, separate from Safari, so the first launch is always a sign-in. That sign-in then happens outside the app's scope: Android shows a browser URL bar, and iOS can open it in an in-app browser sheet, which may not hand its session back to the app. Separately, someone who taps "Add to Home Screen" on the sign-in page gets a plain bookmark with no manifest and no icon.
   *Impact:* High · *Effort:* M · *Owner:* UI (Backend only needs to confirm the redirect targets).

3. **Show employees their last known shifts when they're offline.**
   *What:* When Employee Home or My time goes offline, keep showing the last released view of the person's **own** shifts and clock status, marked "Offline · as of 7:42 AM" (the notification inbox already uses this `Freshness` pattern). Don't clear it. Hold the snapshot in memory, and optionally keep one encrypted "next 7 days, own shifts only" copy that `logout.ts` cleanup wipes. Never cache other people's data.
   *Why:* Earlier QA measured this at 390px (`qa-out/h4/res-home-390.json`). Going offline cut Home's text from 1,030 to 167 characters and its shift times from 7 to 0. Timecards went from 381 to 218 characters and showed "You're offline. Reconnect to see timecards". The Schedule page kept everything, because it is server-rendered. So the same data stays on one page and disappears on another. The cause is `employee-home-reviewed.tsx:263-266`, where `paused` sets `released = null`. The offline page even tells people to "Reconnect to see your schedule". For an hourly worker on weak signal, "when is my next shift" is the main reason to open the app.
   *Impact:* High · *Effort:* M · *Owner:* UI; Product signs off the privacy rule for shared phones.

4. **Make every push show something, and stop sending to suppressed devices.**
   *What:* On the server, stop delivery to any subscription whose ownership generation is suppressed or logged out. In the worker, stop returning silently. Show a neutral, low-detail fallback ("Open NEVORA to check for updates") or none of this at all, but only after the server guarantees it won't send.
   *Why:* `public/fuel-portal-sw.js:169` returns without `showNotification` whenever the envelope fails validation or this browser is not the owner. Subscriptions are created with `userVisibleOnly: true` (`device-client.ts:353`), which is a promise to show a notification for every push. Chrome then shows its own "This site has been updated in the background" notice. Safari and iOS can revoke a subscription after repeated silent pushes. Because ownership hand-off on shared devices is an intended flow, silent drops will happen in real use, and the result looks like "notifications randomly stop on iPhone".
   *Impact:* High · *Effort:* M · *Owner:* Backend (delivery filter), then UI (worker fallback).

5. **Build a real "new version" flow.**
   *What:* (a) Expose the build ID, for example in a `<meta>` tag or a tiny `/app/api/version` endpoint, and check it when the app comes back to the foreground (`visibilitychange`) or every 30 minutes. When it differs, show a quiet toast: "NEVORA updated · Refresh". If a form has unsaved changes, wait until it is saved before showing it. (b) Set `deploymentId` in `next.config.ts`, or use the host's skew protection, so a stale client calling a server action from the 16 `"use server"` files after a deploy gets a clean reload instead of an error. (c) In `src/app/app/error.tsx`, run `window.location.reload()` on `ChunkLoadError` or server-action-mismatch errors. Today `reset()` (line 20) re-renders the same stale client. (d) Rewrite "Close NEVORA tabs and reopen" (`portal-device-setup.tsx:65`): on an iPhone Home Screen app there are no tabs.
   *Why:* An installed app on iOS can stay alive for days. The only update signal today is `UPDATE_WAITING`, and it covers just the worker script. It is sent only to push-enrolled users (`device-client.ts:364`) and appears only inside the closed profile popover. Nothing anywhere checks the app's own build.
   *Impact:* High · *Effort:* M · *Owner:* UI; Backend for `deploymentId` and the hosting setup.

6. **Refresh server-rendered pages when the app is reopened.**
   *What:* Add one listener in the shell. When the page becomes visible after more than 5 minutes hidden, call `router.refresh()`, unless a form has unsaved changes. Optionally add pull-to-refresh on Schedule only, in standalone mode.
   *Why:* An iOS Home Screen app has no reload button and no pull-to-refresh. Polling pages already refresh on focus: 28 files listen for `visibilitychange`, including manager-today, the action centre and timecards. Schedule (manager and employee), Team, Requests/Availability, Updates and Settings do not. A manager who reopens the app in the afternoon sees the morning's schedule, and the only fix is to force-quit the app.
   *Impact:* Medium-High · *Effort:* S · *Owner:* UI.

7. **Bring the offline page up to v3 and make it bilingual.**
   *What:* In `public/fuel-portal-offline.html`, add Spanish (choose it from `navigator.language`, or show both), use the system font stack instead of Inter, add a `prefers-color-scheme: dark` version, and set the tint from the v3 token. Retry automatically on the `online` event. "Try again" should reload the address the person was trying to open, not always `/app`. Replace the 371 KB of inline base64 PNGs with an inline SVG wordmark. Build `CACHE_NAME` from a content hash so that an edited offline page actually reaches devices: today it changes only when someone remembers to bump `v4` (`sw.js:9`). In `error.tsx`, show "You're offline" copy when `navigator.onLine === false` instead of "This page could not be loaded".
   *Why:* The file has `lang="en"` with English-only copy, Inter, and the old `#077572` teal (lines 2, 10-27). The worker's first-install precache is about 985 KB: the offline page (371 KB), the wordmark (120 KB) and the icons (493 KB, of which `fuel-512.png` is 243 KB). That is a lot for a prepaid data plan.
   *Impact:* Medium · *Effort:* S · *Owner:* UI.

8. **Make tapping a notification safe, and make notifications more useful.**
   *What:* In `notificationclick`, don't call `client.navigate()` on the first portal tab (`sw.js:187`), because that throws away any unsaved form in that tab. Focus the tab and `postMessage` the target instead, so the app routes with its own unsaved-changes guard, and open a new window only if no tab exists. Add a monochrome `badge` icon for the Android status bar. Use `navigator.setAppBadge(unread)` from the existing inbox count. Translate the titles, and use sentence case to match the voice ("Your schedule changed", not "Your NEVORA Schedule Changed", `sw.js:150-152`). The worker checks the title and body strings exactly, so Spanish copy has to be added inside the worker too.
   *Impact:* Medium · *Effort:* S-M · *Owner:* UI; Backend for the localised payload.

9. **Tighten the manifest and set the theme colour by role.**
   *What:* Set `theme-color` per page: on Owner and Manager v3 pages use the light grouped background, and keep the dark masthead only for Employee and Lead (`layout.tsx:43-45` currently sends `#1C4A47` to everyone). Add a `media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)"` variant. Set `background_color` to match the first screen, so there is no white splash before a grey page. Add `lang`/`dir`, `description`, a few `screenshots` (Chrome uses them for its richer install sheet), and `launch_handler: {client_mode: "focus-existing"}` so shortcuts and notifications don't open duplicate windows. If workspace lookup ever depends on the session cookie (the tenancy work), add `crossorigin="use-credentials"` to the manifest link, because browsers fetch manifests without cookies.
   *Why:* `manifest.ts:15-31`. In the AUDIT-M-home-390 screenshot the v3 page is light grey, but the browser and status bar would be dark teal, which clashes with the native-iOS look the spec approved.
   *Impact:* Low-Medium · *Effort:* S · *Owner:* UI.

10. **App stores: use a TWA for Android, and Capacitor for iOS only if store listing becomes a goal.**
    *What:* For Android, wrap the existing PWA as a TWA with Bubblewrap. It's cheap, but it needs `/.well-known/assetlinks.json` (`public/` has none). For iOS, there is no TWA. Use a thin Capacitor shell with native APNs push behind the same `DeviceRuntime` interface, so the ownership and suppression logic is reused. That also removes the "install to the Home Screen before notifications work" step for iPhone users. Don't rewrite the app in React Native or Expo. A plain web wrapper risks rejection under Apple's guideline 4.2, so the iOS shell needs native push to justify itself.
    *Impact:* Low now, rising after launch · *Effort:* M (Android), L (iOS) · *Owner:* Product decides; UI builds.

## What's good
- Permission is requested only from an explicit tap, synchronously inside the click handler (`device-client.ts:~104`). iOS shows "install first" before it asks, and the auto-offer opens at most once and only when nothing has focus (`portal-device-setup.tsx:120-125`). Lock-screen notification copy is fixed and checked by the worker, so no schedule details leak.
- The worker is conservative in the right ways. It handles navigations only, never caches signed-in HTML, RSC or API responses, does not call `skipWaiting` or `claim` (so nothing reloads mid-edit), and has a `RETIRE` switch to turn itself off.
- Phone edges are handled well. The tab bar, sheets and Clocker footer respect the home-indicator safe area (`mobile-workforce.css:90`, `premium-clean-manager.css:98`). `viewport-fit=cover`, maskable icons and an apple-touch-icon are set, and Android shortcuts drop when a module is off.

## Not checked / limits
- I had no browser and no device (my brief excludes browser use). I could not check, on a real iPhone or Android phone: install prompts, how an out-of-scope `/login` behaves in standalone mode, swipe-back, status-bar colour, splash screens, or whether push still arrives after silent drops. Platform behaviour in recommendations 2, 4 and 9 comes from known browser rules, not from observation here.
- I did not know whether production has push configured, and that decides how severe recommendation 1 is. I ran no Lighthouse installability check and no vitest suites.
- The offline numbers are from earlier QA (`qa-out/h4`, Employee at 390px) and were not re-measured. I did not inspect the Clocker kiosk's offline behaviour beyond reading the code. It keeps an idempotent recovery record but has no offline punch queue, and adding one is a Product decision.
