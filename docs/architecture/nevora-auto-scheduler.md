## Inputs and shared rules

`src/lib/data/schedule-generation-snapshot.ts` reads the complete scheduling source in one MVCC SQL statement, validates the actor/workspace/week/scope and maps persisted facts into `GenerationInput`. The workspace timezone, active catalogs, explicit readiness, employee eligibility/positions, normal and submitted availability, exceptions, approved leave, configured targets/maximums, policy and complete cross-location dated ledger are authoritative. There is no customer-name, location-count or machine-timezone fallback. Prior/next-workweek commitments participate when their dates touch relevant periods; owning schedule identity remains separate from business date.

`src/lib/scheduling/generation/` contains pure deterministic planning. It derives demand with existing readiness and schedule-health functions, enumerates configured periods and evaluates candidates with the shared `validateShift` AUTOMATIC mode. No second eligibility engine or replacement availability model is introduced. Inactive/missing setup, qualification, cross-location overlaps, approved leave, explicit unavailability, daily constraints and maximum weekly hours cannot be bypassed. Missing confirmation is a warning/ranking fact; target hours are preferences, never hard maximums. Scheduled hours use the existing nominal business-wall-minute ledger, not Clocker worked hours.

Coverage uses the maximum staffing and maximum per-position quantity across overlapping configured windows, rather than adding duplicate requirements. Distinct people provide distinct capacity. One shift has at most one assigned position; designated Lead is an independent overlay and may coexist with that person's position. Fully satisfied configured minutes determine the displayed coverage percentage. Missing attempted configured periods means coverage unavailable, not invented 100%; unready locations remain separate setup exclusions.

## Search, fairness and budgets

The planner prioritizes constrained needs and scarce legal candidates, retains the best legal partial, uses bounded backtracking and caches employee validation against the actual ledger. Confirmed availability ranks first among otherwise equivalent candidates; employees below positive configured targets rank next. Target-completion ratio applies only when both employees are below positive targets, followed by remaining unmet target, actual scheduled weekly minutes and a stable rotating week tie. Absent/zero targets acquire no invented ratio or universal penalty. Input array order does not choose the result. No randomness, wall-clock stopping or optimality promise is used.

Production service limits are 20,000 nodes, 200,000 evaluator calls and 10,000 cumulative periods. They cannot be overridden through the action. Budget exhaustion retains a legal partial and explicit diagnostics. Counter limits include final legality checks; cache hits are separately reported. Prior local deterministic benchmarks measured 50 workers/3 locations at 769 ms (42 additions, 43 nodes, 5,499 evaluations, 882 periods), and a deliberately dense 20-location fixture at 33,028 ms (280 additions, 281 nodes, 86,750 evaluations, 39,200 periods). The dense fixture explicitly allowed 100,000 periods and configured two-hour coverage; it is not the service default or a hosting-latency claim. ## Atomic write protocol

`generateWeekDraft` is the single retry owner. Each attempt reads, authorizes, maps and solves fresh source outside the transaction, then submits one ordered final guard/write batch. At most three full attempts retry named source conflicts, serialization failures or deadlocks. Lock/statement timeout becomes a typed busy/conflict response. Permission/scope failures and unknown commit outcomes are not blindly retried.

The final batch sets bounded lock/statement timeouts (1.5 s/5 s), takes the singleton workspace `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` first, sorted owning/affected-week advisory locks, then deterministic parent-before-child SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE input-table guards. A separate fresh source comparison runs after lock waits. Canonical scoped Draft writes and an exact after-state assertion complete the batch. Actual PostgreSQL barriers prove both writer orders, absent-row inserts and cascade protection. No-op/invalid outcomes still revalidate current access and source through the final batch. Broad source reads and brief table serialization are deliberate conservative limitations; solver work does not hold these locks.

Generation does not call the publication mutation helper, rewrite unrelated published history, create acknowledgement/publication revisions or notification intents, or contact providers. Draft-only changes commit atomically or roll back. Existing published manual changes retain their original atomic revision/intent path.
