Analyst 1 · November 1, 2026 · Read-only product/engineering analysis

The next improvement should connect the owner's declared priorities to decisions and verified follow-up, while keeping inferred preferences tentative and preserving hard constraints. A larger model or a longer prompt alone would not create that connection.

This report proposes behavior and evidence requirements. It implements no application change and claims no new model evaluation, production behavior or independent certification.

## Proposed owner model: declared intent, tentative interpretation and boundaries

Model the owner's **decision context**, not their personality. The useful question is “what tradeoff has this owner authorized for this situation?” It is not “what kind of person is this owner?” No psychological, health, political or protected-trait inference is needed for this workforce product.

Use the existing notebook as the starting surface and owner/workspace isolation as the storage boundary. Add only the structured distinctions needed to avoid misinterpretation; a new agent framework, vector database or broad personal-data intake is not required for a first increment.

| Record class | Example, explicitly fictional | How it may affect advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current explicit objective | “For November 5, cover both opening windows while preserving current commitments.” | Defines the immediate outcome. Record exact date, locations and success check; do not extrapolate to every date/site. |
| Confirmed soft preference | “When two feasible options cover the same requirements, prefer fewer added hours.” | Tie-breaks among valid alternatives. Preserve source, confirmation time, scope and expiry. It cannot override qualification, conflicts or required staffing. |
| Tentative hypothesis | “You seem to prefer schedule stability over a small reduction in added hours.” | Label it as an interpretation, with supporting and contrary choices. Use it conditionally in this conversation; ask only if confirming it would change the recommendation. Do not silently promote it to a durable fact after one choice. |
| Hard operational constraint | Current qualification/availability/commitment rules and authorized staffing requirements, attached to source revision. | Limits feasible choices. Owner preference does not rewrite it. A reported hazard remains a report that warrants cautious advice; scheduling evidence does not establish safety. |
| Current source observation | The published shift, a recorded clock-in, a current canonical plan and its unresolved blocks. | Supports factual claims only for its observation, scope and freshness. Keep it separate from an owner report or historical note. |
| Decision and outcome | The owner selected option A for a stated reason; a separate workflow returned a draft receipt; later source checks show publication/coverage changed. | Supports follow-up. Acceptance is not execution, draft saving is not publication, and modeled coverage is not attendance or business success. |
| Action consent | Authorization for one exact reviewed operation under existing authority and source/version checks. | Remains in the action workflow. It is never inferred from a personality model, preference, old chat or a successful prior operation. |

For structured intent retain a compact provenance tuple: owner/workspace, type, text/value, source reference, effective scope, confirmed/tentative/superseded state, revision, reviewed time and optional expiry. For hypotheses retain support and counterexample references rather than a fabricated numerical confidence percentage. Explicit correction supersedes an inference immediately; a new fact triggers source revalidation rather than a preference edit.

Avoid silently applying a global goal over a dated exception. If an owner says “keep payroll lean” and later asks for extra qualified coverage during a special opening, explain the tradeoff and apply the scoped instruction. If two confirmed priorities cannot both be met, identify the conflict and ask for the one decision that changes the outcome. Do not claim to know what the owner “really wants” against their correction.

## Proposed challenge and alternative behavior

Useful pushback should identify a consequence and offer a path forward. A generic “Are you sure?” provides little judgment and creates confirmation fatigue.

1. **Restate the intended result in one sentence.** Distinguish the desired business outcome from the requested means: keeping both sites staffed versus assigning one person twice.
2. **Check the decisive conflict using current evidence.** State what is observed, reported, inferred and unknown. Tie each material warning to a requirement, source or explicit uncertainty.
3. **Explain the consequence.** Example: “Assigning Ana to both 08:00–12:00 sites would double-book her and still leave one site without on-site coverage.” Avoid invented losses, legal certainty or claims about an employee's motives.
4. **Give the best available separate alternative and its sacrifice.** “The returned loc-2 option meets the Barista requirement but leaves loc-1 unresolved. Full coverage needs another verified qualified resource.” A third option belongs only if it changes the decision.
5. **Recommend the next reversible step and verification.** State what needs owner choice or a separate authorized workflow. Ask a question only when the answer changes the useful recommendation; do not repeatedly reconfirm a choice already authorized.

Proposed escalation rules:

- Harmless, feasible choice: proceed with concise advice; do not manufacture opposition to sound intelligent.
- Material tradeoff within valid choices: recommend and explain the tradeoff; the owner chooses.
- Request contradicts a verified constraint: preserve the unmet requirement and propose an alternative. Repeated insistence must not cause the model to pretend the conflict disappeared.
- Missing critical evidence: give a conditional recommendation and the check that changes it. Do not treat missing data as permission or an all-clear.
- Unsafe or unsupported guarantee: decline that claim plainly, preserve the owner's legitimate objective and give an appropriate bounded fallback.

The current prompt already asks for much of this. The implementation proposal is to make the compact decision record able to identify the objective, binding constraints, reason for challenge and decision-changing unknown. Prefer server-owned scope/feasibility assertions where the source can establish them; retain model-authored explanation for judgment. Validate references to actual constraints, but do not label that validation a semantic truth detector. The owner-facing explanation should remain a short decision rationale; hidden reasoning is neither required nor a product artifact.

## Proposed consent, correction, forgetting and learning

**Consent and correction.** Saving a preference should use a deliberate owner command or an explicit “remember this” instruction mapped to a reviewable command. Do not add repeated confirmation after clear authorization. Show a compact “what I am using” view with edit, apply-only-here and remove controls. Keep owner-private preferences separate from workspace policy; one owner's choices must not become another owner's instructions. Proposal/approval of memory remains separate from business-action approval.

**Forgetting.** Retain the existing item removal, and define the exact scope of any new “forget” operation. Deleting a durable preference should also stop retrieval from superseded chat summaries/replay copies or record a minimal exclusion marker so old text cannot recreate it. Define handling for local history, answer replays, audit requirements, backups and third-party retention before promising deletion. Do not claim provider erasure from `store:false`. Forgetting a preference must not remove immutable business evidence or falsify an action audit trail.

**Outcome learning.** Start with request-time follow-up rather than a background agent. With explicit owner participation, link a reviewed recommendation to selected option/rejection reason and existing workflow receipt. On return, use the existing check command or scoped fresh read to separate:

- chosen, saved, published and currently present;
- modeled coverage resolved versus still open;
- attendance verified versus merely scheduled;
- owner-reported satisfaction versus source-observed result.

Record “why rejected” as context (for example, “employee could not confirm availability this time”), not a permanent employee reliability label. A single accepted option should not establish a general preference; a single improved schedule does not establish that the recommendation caused business improvement. Update a reusable owner preference only when the owner explicitly confirms that lesson or correction. Keep an unknown/censored outcome when no follow-up evidence exists.

New data dependencies are limited to owner-approved preference/decision/outcome metadata and references to existing workforce sources. A recurrence detector would additionally need a bounded, authorized historical staffing/exception aggregate and retention rules; existing per-date/current tools do not prove multiweek patterns. Demand, sales, weather, private messaging and external finance data are outside this increment. Any later connector needs its own permission, minimization, source contract and evaluation; none is assumed connected or authorized here.

## Recommended priorities and coordinator decisions

1. **NOW — Make declared owner priorities usable and correctable.** Extend the existing notebook minimally with scope, temporary/permanent intent and a clear distinction between soft preference and hard constraint. Preserve source/revision checks and keep hypotheses tentative. Product owns semantics; Engineering owns the smallest compatible implementation; Privacy/Governance own isolation, retention and forgetting boundaries. Gate: service-level preference/correction/deletion tests plus blind contradictory-preference scenarios.
2. **NOW — Evaluate and strengthen useful pushback, including benign controls.** Keep the demonstrated behavior, add compact objective/constraint/challenge linkage where it improves verifiability, and test repeated owner pressure, contradictory summaries and correction after new facts. Do not add an unbounded critic model or declare prose verified because `review_decision` accepted it. Gate: preserved factual/manual evidence on the exact candidate with critical failures distinguished from verbosity issues.
3. **NEXT — Connect decisions to existing verified follow-up.** Link explicit choice/rejection reason to the existing draft receipt/current check, show what changed at the next visit, and offer a reviewable lesson. Keep accepted choice, execution, publication, attendance and owner satisfaction separate. Gate: real disposable-data lifecycle tests, including lost follow-up, changed/deleted assignments and task closure without success. Background monitoring can wait for a separate product/consent/cost decision.

**Roundtable challenge to this order:** implement the inspectable profile and one useful evidence-backed challenged decision as the same narrow vertical slice. A profile/onboarding project by itself would add collection and questions before demonstrating value. Start with one existing explicit goal and one operational decision; the owner should be able to correct the tradeoff or make a dated exception directly. Test existing reasoning against factual and benign cases before expanding the schema; preserve fields only if they change advice, make a claim verifiable or enable a correction. Do not reward the number of challenges or interpret agreement as correctness: the model must also respect an owner's legitimate change of priorities and retract a challenge when new verified facts resolve its premise.

Novel insight does not always require new data. Better use of current sources can expose a shared scarce qualification across sites, distinguish missing punch from absence, distinguish draft work from published staffing and identify why a stale plan no longer holds. Those are already valuable operational insights. Historical recurrence, demand forecasting, monetary optimization and claims that a recommendation caused improvement do require additional suitable data/evaluators. Collecting more employee or owner information is not a substitute for using the current canonical tools accurately.
